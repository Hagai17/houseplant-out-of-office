## Duel scoring (judge's criteria, in order)

All valid drafts pass criterion 1 (each reads as a houseplant's OOO reply), so the
duels turn on criteria 2–3: fresh surprise, and the plant's life driving the joke.

- C3 vs C12: both dry cactus deadpan. C3's "whenever someone remembers I exist"
  has the neglect engine; C12's "the office was never its idea" is a shrug.
  C3 wins, but both are gentle — neither has a real twist.
- C7 vs C8: C7 leans on the worn phrase "regrets nothing" (criterion 5 hurts it).
  C8 builds "out of office / out of water / out of patience" and twists
  "will not be returning. In any sense." — returning a reply vs returning to
  life. C8 wins.
- C4 vs C11: C4's "contact the sun — it also ignores you, but with more
  authority" is a good single joke. C11's "converting your neglect into oxygen;
  replies resume when the watering does" makes the plant's whole life the
  mechanism (criterion 3). C11 wins.
- C2b vs C1: C2b is elegant — "one of those facts is about to become permanent"
  — but the laugh needs a beat of thought. C1 stacks two punches: the fake
  office form "Expected return: never" and then the blunt reveal "I'm a fern,
  and I'm dead." Faster, bigger surprise, tighter (103 vs 116 chars). C1 wins.
- Final — C1 vs C11: C11 is the cleverest wordplay, but its laugh lands soft.
  C1's deadpan death reveal is the strongest surprise in the field, the neglect
  (no water since March) is the entire engine of the joke, and at 103 bytes it
  is also lean for the smaller-text tie-break. C1 wins.
